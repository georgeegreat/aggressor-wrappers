feat(coverage): a prediction over part of a sequence is a prediction about that part

Both of this project's RPS12 files cover residues 10-129 of a 132-residue
chain. Refused, the protein lost two predictors from its panel. Accepted at
face value, each file aligns at residue 1 and every call lands nine residues
early -- a complete, plausible, wrong answer that nothing downstream could
detect. Neither is the right answer.

A short file is now accepted when its placement can be ESTABLISHED, and the
residues outside it are marked not predicted rather than predicted-negative.
Those are different claims: a zero says the tool looked and found nothing, and
collapsing the two inflates every denominator computed from the track. The
marker is NaN, which survives the parsed CSV as a blank cell, the merged table,
and the amyloscope export, so nothing in the chain needed a new column.

Placement is established two ways and guessed neither.

FoldAmyloid reports residue identities, so it places itself: the observed
string is matched against the sequence and accepted only if it occurs at
exactly one offset. Several means the sequence repeats and the file cannot be
placed; none means it is not this protein's, which is the mis-filed case the
identity check already caught and whose wording is kept, since that is what
every caller recognises it by. The parser now also reads the file on its OWN
axis first and places the block afterwards -- it used to write straight into a
sequence-length array, so a file covering 10-129 landed at 1-120 before
anything could notice.

WALTZ's .dat carries positions and scores and no identities at all, so nothing
inside a short file can place it: 120 lines against a 132-residue chain is
equally consistent with residues 1-120 and with 13-132, and the two readings
put every segment twelve residues apart. The offset is therefore declared, per
protein, in [predictors.waltz] offsets -- a statement about what was SUBMITTED,
which only the submitter knows. Per protein by design: one offset applied to a
whole panel would silently shift every protein whose file was in fact complete,
which is this machinery's own failure mode reintroduced one level up.

Declaring it exposed a gap of its own. The pipeline parses through the RUNNER,
not through get_parser(), so a [predictors.waltz] key that only the parser
reads was validated at load and then ignored by every route except the
single-file CLI. The runner carries the offsets now, and the same forwarding
was added for FoldAmyloid's use_tool_flag and accept_partial.

On the panel this came from, all fourteen saved files are now usable: WALTZ
seven of seven with RPS12 declared, FoldAmyloid seven of seven once its RPL27
and RPS28 exports are swapped back into their own names. RPS12's APR moves from
64-68 as the file numbers it to 73-77 where it belongs, and twelve residues in
each of its two tracks are blank rather than zero.

Three guards earn their place in passing: a profile with holes in it is refused
(it cannot be placed against anything), a profile longer than the sequence is
refused as a different protein rather than reported as "14 of 12 positions",
and partial acceptance can be switched off with accept_partial=False for a run
that wants to require complete files.

Eighteen tests. 558 pass, 7 skip.
